# Content Pipeline

## Overview

Most content programs fail not on writing quality but on the *pipeline*: pieces get published with no keyword target, no distribution plan, and no measurement, so nobody can say whether they worked. This skill runs content as an assembly line where every stage has an owner, an input, and an exit gate.

**Core principle:** No piece advances a stage until the prior stage's exit gate is met. A draft with no brief is guesswork; a brief with no keyword is a hunch; a publish with no distribution plan is a tree falling in an empty forest. The gates are what make the pipeline repeatable instead of heroic.

## When to use

- Standing up or fixing a content marketing program with a regular cadence.
- Content ships but doesn't rank, get read, or convert.
- Writers, SEO, and design are stepping on each other with no clear handoffs.

## When NOT to use

- **One-off announcement or launch copy** — no keyword research or ranking horizon; write and ship.
- **Technical documentation** — different quality bar and audience; use a docs workflow.
- **Diagnosing why existing pages don't rank** — audit first (see seo-audit), then feed findings here.

## The workflow

```
STEP 1        STEP 2      STEP 3     STEP 4        STEP 5      STEP 6          STEP 7
keyword    →  brief   →  draft   →  editorial  →  publish  →  distribute  →  measure
research      (spec)               review                     (amplify)      (KPIs)
```

### STEP 1 — Keyword research

For each candidate topic, capture: search volume, search intent (informational / commercial / transactional), keyword difficulty, and SERP features present (featured snippet, PAA, video). Reject topics with no realistic ranking path or no business relevance.
**Exit:** a shortlist of target keywords, each with volume, intent, difficulty, and SERP features recorded.

### STEP 2 — Content brief

Write a brief BEFORE any drafting. It must contain: the single primary keyword, the angle/thesis, a heading outline, source material and citations to use, target internal links, and the intent the piece must satisfy.
**Exit:** a brief exists with a primary keyword, angle, outline, sources, and internal-link targets. A writer could start from it without asking questions.

### STEP 3 — Draft

Write to the brief. Match the SERP intent (a "best X" query wants a comparison, not an essay). Hit the outline; use the internal links; cite the sources.
**Exit:** a complete draft that covers the outline, targets the primary keyword naturally, and includes the planned internal links.

### STEP 4 — Editorial review

Review against four axes, each an explicit pass/fail: **clarity** (readable, structured), **accuracy** (claims sourced, facts checked), **SEO** (title/H1/meta/keyword/links present and correct), and **brand voice** (matches the style guide). Return with tracked changes, don't rewrite silently.
**Exit:** all four axes pass; reviewer sign-off recorded.

### STEP 5 — Publish

Publish with: final title + meta description, canonical URL, structured data if applicable, images with alt text, and internal links live. Confirm it renders and is indexable.
**Exit:** the piece is live at its canonical URL, indexable, and renders correctly on mobile and desktop.

### STEP 6 — Distribute

A publish is the start, not the finish. Amplify across: social (owned channels), email (newsletter/segment), and outreach (relevant communities, partners, or link targets). Schedule, don't just "post once."
**Exit:** a distribution plan is executed or scheduled across at least social + email, with dates and owners.

### STEP 7 — Measure

Define KPIs before publishing and check them on a schedule: rankings (target keyword position over time), traffic (organic + referral), engagement (scroll/time/shares), and conversions (signups, demos, revenue-attributed). Set a review date.
**Exit:** KPIs are instrumented and tracked; a review meeting is scheduled (monthly for velocity, quarterly for strategy).

## Common Pitfalls

- **Drafting before briefing.** A writer starting without a brief produces a piece the SEO lead has to reverse-engineer a keyword onto. The brief is the cheapest stage to change; skipping it makes every later stage expensive.
- **Treating publish as the finish line.** Great content with no distribution reaches nobody. Budget as much attention for STEP 6 as for STEP 3.
- **Vanity metrics.** Pageviews feel good and predict nothing. Tie the piece to a conversion or a ranking, or you can't tell winners from noise.
- **Intent mismatch.** Writing a 3,000-word guide for a query where Google ranks product pages means you'll never rank. Match the SERP.
- **No cadence.** Content published in unpredictable bursts never compounds. Pick a monthly velocity you can actually sustain and hold it.

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Target keywords shortlisted with volume, intent, difficulty, and SERP features.
- [ ] A brief (primary keyword, angle, outline, sources, internal links) exists before drafting.
- [ ] Draft covers the outline, targets the keyword naturally, and includes planned internal links.
- [ ] Editorial review passed on all four axes (clarity, accuracy, SEO, brand voice) with sign-off.
- [ ] Piece published at canonical URL, indexable, rendering correctly.
- [ ] Distribution executed/scheduled across at least social + email, with owners and dates.
- [ ] KPIs (rankings, traffic, engagement, conversions) instrumented and tracked.
- [ ] Review meeting scheduled (monthly velocity / quarterly strategy).
