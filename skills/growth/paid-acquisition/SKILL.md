# Paid Acquisition

## Overview

Paid acquisition without structure is a machine for setting money on fire: spend spread across too many channels, creative changed on a whim, no target CAC, and no rule for when to kill a losing ad. This skill imposes the discipline — pick channels deliberately, structure campaigns cleanly, test creative as hypotheses, and let pre-committed ROAS/CAC targets decide what scales and what stops.

**Core principle:** Decide the kill/scale rules *before* you spend, not after. An optimization decision made mid-panic on a Friday is a gut decision; a decision made against a pre-set CAC target is a policy. The budget is split into *test* money (buying learning) and *scale* money (buying growth) — never blend the two.

## When to use

- Planning a paid program or a new channel launch.
- Spend is running but not converting, or CAC/ROAS is unknown or drifting.
- Creative testing is ad hoc and nobody can say which variant won or why.

## When NOT to use

- **Organic/content growth** — no media spend; use content-pipeline or seo-audit.
- **Brand campaigns measured on awareness, not response** — different KPIs than the CAC/ROAS loop here.
- **A landing page that doesn't convert organic traffic yet** — fix the page first; paid traffic won't rescue a broken funnel.

## The workflow

```
STEP 1        STEP 2        STEP 3       STEP 4        STEP 5      STEP 6
channel    →  campaign   →  creative  →  LP align  →  budget   →  optimize
selection     structure     testing      + targets     allocate     loop
```

### STEP 1 — Channel selection

Choose channels by where your audience is and the intent they arrive with: **Meta** (broad interest/demand-gen), **Google** (search intent, high-commercial), **LinkedIn** (B2B targeting, higher CPC), and others as they fit. Start with one or two, not five.
**Exit:** 1–2 primary channels chosen with a written rationale tying each to audience and intent.

### STEP 2 — Campaign structure

Build a clean hierarchy — campaign → ad group/ad set → ad → creative — with one variable per level so results are attributable. Naming convention enforced. One audience or theme per ad set.
**Exit:** the account structure follows a consistent hierarchy and naming convention; each ad set isolates one audience/theme.

### STEP 3 — Creative testing framework

Test creative as hypotheses, not vibes: **hypothesis** (what you believe and why) → **variant** (one changed element) → **measure** (against a pre-set metric and minimum sample) → **iterate** (kill losers, scale winners, form the next hypothesis). Change one thing at a time.
**Exit:** a creative testing schedule exists with hypotheses, isolated variants, success metrics, and a decision rule for each test.

### STEP 4 — Landing-page alignment & targets

Ensure **message match** (the ad's promise appears above the fold on the LP), a single clear **CTA**, and a friction-appropriate **form**. Set the numbers now: target **CAC** and **ROAS** (or CPA), and the payback horizon.
**Exit:** each ad maps to an aligned landing page (message match + CTA verified), and target CAC/ROAS are documented before launch.

### STEP 5 — Budget allocation (test vs scale)

Split budget explicitly: a **test** pool (buying learning on new channels/creative/audiences) and a **scale** pool (pouring into proven winners). Allocate across channels and campaigns by expected and then actual efficiency. Never let unvalidated bets draw from scale money.
**Exit:** budget is documented as a test/scale split, allocated by channel and campaign, with a cap on test spend per experiment.

### STEP 6 — Optimization loop

Run cadenced reviews: **daily** (spend pacing, obvious breakage), **weekly** (creative/audience performance vs. targets), **monthly** (channel mix, budget reallocation). Apply pre-set **scale** rules (winner beats target with significance → move to scale pool) and **stop** rules (loser misses target past its test cap → kill). Feed learnings back to STEP 3.
**Exit:** review cadence is running and scale/stop decisions are being made against pre-set CAC/ROAS rules, not ad hoc.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Spreading budget across too many channels.** Five channels at low spend each gives five inconclusive results. Concentrate until a channel is proven, then expand.
- **Testing multiple variables at once.** Change the headline *and* the image and you learn nothing about either. One variable per test, always.
- **No pre-set kill rule.** Without a stop rule, losers run "just a bit longer" indefinitely on hope. Set the CAC threshold and test cap up front and honor them.
- **Blaming creative when the landing page is the leak.** A great ad to a mismatched page converts nobody. Verify message match before iterating on ads.
- **Blending test and scale budget.** Funding unproven bets from scale money starves winners and hides which spend is buying growth vs. learning. Keep the pools separate.

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] 1–2 primary channels chosen with written rationale (audience + intent).
- [ ] Account structured in a consistent hierarchy with a naming convention; one variable isolated per level.
- [ ] Creative testing schedule with hypotheses, one-variable variants, success metrics, and decision rules.
- [ ] Every ad mapped to an aligned landing page (message match + single CTA verified).
- [ ] Target CAC and ROAS (and payback horizon) documented before launch.
- [ ] Budget split into test vs scale pools, allocated by channel/campaign, with a per-experiment test cap.
- [ ] Optimization cadence (daily/weekly/monthly) running with pre-set scale/stop rules driving decisions.
