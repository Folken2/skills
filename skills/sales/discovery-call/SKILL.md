# Discovery Call

## Overview

A discovery call earns the right to a deal. Its output is not rapport — it is a *qualified, quantified, mapped* opportunity: a documented problem, its business impact in numbers, who decides, and how they decide.

**Core principle:** Diagnose before you prescribe. A rep who demos before quantifying pain is a vendor pitching features; a rep who quantifies pain first is a consultant the buyer wants to keep talking to. **People buy emotionally and justify logically** (Sandler) — surface the emotional pain, then hand them the numbers to justify it.

The discipline here is *question-to-pitch ratio*: in discovery you are mostly listening. If you are talking more than the prospect, you are not discovering.

## When to use

- A qualified lead (see **lead-qualification**) is booked for a first/discovery call.
- A deal stalled and you need to re-discover what actually changed.
- You're inheriting a deal and need to reconstruct problem, impact, and decision map.

## When NOT to use

- **Unqualified inbound triage** — score and route first with **lead-qualification**; don't burn a discovery slot on a bad-fit lead.
- **Late-stage negotiation / procurement** — discovery is done; you're on paper process now. Use MEDDIC's Paper Process, not this.
- **Pure demo requested by an already-educated buyer** — still open with 5 minutes of confirm-the-problem discovery, but don't force the full arc.

## Methodology sources

This skill blends established methodologies — attribute them, don't reinvent:

| Layer | Method | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Question architecture | **SPIN** (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff) | Neil Rackham, *SPIN Selling* |
| Deepening pain | **Pain Funnel** (8 nested questions to quantified pain) | David Sandler / Sandler Training |
| Qualification / deal map | **MEDDIC** (Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion) | Dick Dunkel / Jack Napoli (PTC) |

Working pattern: SPIN Situation+Problem to anchor → Sandler funnel to deepen Problem into quantified Pain → SPIN Implication+Need-payoff to transfer value → capture the rest of MEDDIC.

## The Workflow

```
BEFORE            DURING (the call)                                    AFTER
pre-call research  →  problem → amplify → quantify → budget →          post-call
                      authority → timeline → decision criteria         debrief
```

### 0 — Pre-call research (before the call)

Never open cold. Arrive knowing:

- **Company:** what they do, size, recent news, funding, hiring signals, obvious pressures.
- **Person:** role, tenure, likely goals and metrics they own.
- **Hypothesis:** 1–2 problems you believe they have and why. You'll test these, not assert them.
- **Their trigger:** why did *this* lead raise a hand *now*? (from lead-qualification data)

If you can't state a problem hypothesis, do more research before dialing.

### 1 — Problem identification (SPIN: Situation → Problem)

Open with a light agenda and permission, then ask situation questions to confirm context, and problem questions to surface difficulties. Let them describe the problem in *their* words. Do not pitch. Log the problem verbatim.

### 2 — Need amplification (Sandler Pain Funnel)

Take the surfaced problem from generic to specific to emotional using nested questions:
- "Tell me more about that…" → "Can you give me a specific example?" → "How long has this been going on?" → "What have you tried?" → "Did that work?" → "How does that make you feel?" → "What happens if nothing changes?"

Stay in the pain. The instinct to relieve it with your solution is the enemy of discovery.

### 3 — Impact quantification (SPIN: Implication)

Convert pain into numbers — this is the metric that later justifies the purchase:
- Cost of the problem: hours, headcount, churned revenue, missed pipeline, error rate × cost.
- Frequency and trend (getting worse?).
- Get the prospect to state or agree to the number. *Their* number, not yours.

If you leave with no number, you have a conversation, not an opportunity.

### 4 — Budget exploration

Explore money without a blunt "what's your budget." Anchor to the quantified impact from step 3: "If this is costing ~$X/quarter, is there budget allocated to fixing it, or would this need to be built into a case?" Capture: budget exists / must be created / no budget. Note the buying process for funds.

### 5 — Authority mapping (MEDDIC: Economic Buyer + Champion)

Map the humans:
- **Economic buyer** — who can say yes and release funds (often not your contact).
- **Champion** — who has power *and* sells for you internally when you're not in the room.
- **Other stakeholders / blockers** — influencers, users, procurement, security.
Ask: "Besides yourself, who else weighs in on a decision like this?" and "How does something like this typically get approved here?"

### 6 — Timeline

Establish a *compelling event* and dates: when do they need this solved, and why that date (contract end, fiscal year, board mandate, launch)? A timeline with no reason behind it is a wish, not a timeline — probe for the driver.

### 7 — Decision criteria & process (MEDDIC: Decision Criteria + Process)

- **Criteria:** what they'll evaluate on (technical must-haves, security, price, integration, references). Whose criteria dominate?
- **Process:** the actual steps from here to signature (evaluation, POC, legal, procurement) and who owns each.
Confirm mutual next step before hanging up. Never end a discovery call without a scheduled next step.

### 8 — Post-call debrief (immediately after)

While it's fresh, fill in call notes using `scripts/discovery-notes-template.md`. Then decide: **advance, disqualify, or nurture** — with the reason. Update the CRM: MEDDIC fields, next step + date, and a crisp "why they would buy" statement.

## Red Flags — STOP

| Thought | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Let me just show them the product" | Demoing before quantifying pain = pitching. Diagnose first. |
| "They said they have a problem, that's enough" | Unquantified pain doesn't fund a purchase. Get the number (step 3). |
| "My main contact will get it approved" | Maybe not the economic buyer. Map authority explicitly (step 5). |
| "They want it soon" | "Soon" isn't a timeline. Find the compelling event and its date. |
| "Great call, we really clicked" | Rapport ≠ opportunity. Do you have pain, metric, buyer, next step? |
| "I'll write up notes later" | Later = lost detail. Debrief immediately with the template. |
| "I did most of the talking, I explained it well" | Discovery is listening. Talk-time over ~50% means you didn't discover. |

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| Dial in cold with no hypothesis | Pre-call research + 1–2 problem hypotheses |
| Relieve the pain with your pitch | Stay in the pain funnel until it's quantified |
| Accept vague pain ("it's a hassle") | Push to a number: cost, frequency, trend |
| Ask "what's your budget?" bluntly | Anchor budget to the quantified impact |
| Assume your contact decides | Map economic buyer, champion, blockers |
| End with "I'll send some info" | End with a scheduled, mutual next step |

## Exit Criteria

The discovery call is complete when ALL are true:

- [ ] Pre-call research done; a problem hypothesis existed before dialing.
- [ ] A specific problem captured **in the prospect's words**.
- [ ] Pain amplified and **quantified with at least one number** (cost / time / frequency).
- [ ] Budget status captured (exists / must be created / none) with the funding path.
- [ ] Economic buyer and champion identified by name/role (or explicitly flagged as unknown-to-find).
- [ ] Timeline captured **with a compelling event / driver**, not just "soon."
- [ ] Decision criteria and the process-to-signature captured.
- [ ] A mutual next step scheduled with a date before the call ended.
- [ ] Notes filled via `scripts/discovery-notes-template.md`; advance/disqualify/nurture decided with a reason; CRM updated.
