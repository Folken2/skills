# Leave Management

## Overview

Leave management is the controlled lifecycle of an employee being away from work — from request through approval, absence, and return. The core principle: **policy is the decision-maker, not the approver.** A clear, written, consistently applied leave policy turns every request from a judgment call into a lookup.

**Classify the leave type first** — the type determines the policy, the pay treatment, the compliance obligations, and the coverage plan. A vacation day and a parental leave share a request form and nothing else. Get the classification wrong and every downstream step is wrong: you apply the wrong policy, mis-pay, miss a statutory notice, and plan the wrong coverage.

**Never let a request sit unacknowledged.** The cost of a silent no is a disengaged employee and an HR complaint. Acknowledge on intake, decide against policy, and record the outcome — even a denial is a documented, explained answer, not silence.

## When to use

- An employee requests time off — vacation/PTO, a sick day, parental leave, bereavement, jury duty, unpaid leave, or a statutory entitlement.
- You need to validate, classify, and approve or deny a leave request against policy.
- You are planning coverage for a known absence, or handling an unplanned/emergency absence after the fact.
- You need to work out the payroll, accrual, and benefits impact of a leave.
- An employee is returning from leave and you need the return-to-work path.
- You are tracking balances, accruals, or absence patterns, or reporting on leave.

## When NOT to use

- The employee is leaving the company (resignation, termination, retirement) → use [[employee-offboarding]]. Leave is a return-to-work event; offboarding is a no-return event.
- Recurring unexplained absence, tardiness, or a performance concern with no valid leave basis → this is an attendance/performance matter, handle via [[employee-engagement]], not the leave lifecycle.
- An ongoing absence that has become a performance or capability concern (e.g., extended sick leave raising fitness-for-duty questions) → involve **HR directly**; this SOP assumes a good-faith, policy-eligible request.
- A contested entitlement, accommodation dispute, or anything with legal exposure → escalate to HR/Legal before deciding.

## Workflow

### 1. Request intake

Capture a complete, timestamped request and **acknowledge it immediately**.

- **Who, what, when:** employee, requested leave type (as they describe it), start date, end date/duration, and whether it's planned or already underway (emergency/retroactive).
- **Notice given:** compare against the notice the policy requires. Short notice doesn't auto-deny, but it flags coverage risk.
- **Acknowledge receipt now** with an expected decision date. Open the leave record here — every later step logs into it (step 9).

### 2. Classify leave type

The classification drives policy, pay, compliance, and coverage. Don't take the employee's label at face value — map it to a policy category.

- **Paid time off / vacation** — discretionary, accrual-funded.
- **Sick leave** — own illness; may be a distinct statutory entitlement.
- **Parental / family** — maternity, paternity, adoption, family caregiving.
- **Bereavement, jury duty, military** — often statutory, usually short.
- **Unpaid leave** — beyond entitlement, or leave of absence by arrangement.
- **Statutory / protected leave** — legally mandated (e.g., FMLA in the US, or local equivalents). If protected leave *might* apply, treat it as protected until confirmed otherwise — under-recognizing it is the costly error.

### 3. Policy check and eligibility

Look the request up against policy — this is where the decision is made.

- **Eligibility:** tenure, hours worked, employment type, and any waiting period for this leave type.
- **Balance / entitlement:** does the request fit available accrued balance or statutory entitlement? Flag negative balances.
- **Policy limits:** blackout periods, maximum consecutive duration, concurrent-leave rules (e.g., protected leave running alongside paid leave).
- **Decision:** approve, deny, or partially approve **strictly on policy**. If denying, cite the specific policy basis and offer alternatives (different dates, unpaid, partial). Record the reasoning.

### 4. Coverage and approval

Route for approval and make the absence operationally safe.

- **Manager approval:** route per the approval chain; the manager owns operational feasibility, policy owns entitlement. Keep the two separate — a manager cannot deny a protected leave for coverage reasons.
- **Coverage plan:** identify who covers critical responsibilities, hand off in-flight work, and set an out-of-office/delegation. No coverage plan = the absence becomes someone's crisis.
- **Confirm to the employee** in writing: approved dates, leave type, pay treatment, and return date.

### 5. Payroll and accruals

Translate the approved leave into correct pay and balance movement.

- **Pay treatment:** paid, partially paid, or unpaid — set by the classified type, not the request.
- **Accrual impact:** deduct used balance; confirm whether accrual continues to accrue *during* the leave (varies by type and jurisdiction).
- **Statutory pay:** apply any mandated leave pay (e.g., statutory sick/parental pay) and any employer top-up per policy.
- **Benefits continuity:** confirm benefits continue during protected/extended leave, and how premiums are handled for unpaid stretches.
- Notify payroll before the cutoff so the correct period reflects it.

### 6. Compliance and documentation

Meet legal obligations and build the audit trail. *Jurisdiction-specific — verify against local law.*

- **Protected-leave designation:** where a statutory leave applies (FMLA or local equivalent), issue the required eligibility/designation notices within the mandated window.
- **Documentation:** collect only what policy/law permits (e.g., medical certification where allowed) — over-collecting is itself a compliance risk.
- **Job protection & non-retaliation:** confirm reinstatement rights and that the absence isn't counted against the employee where protected.
- **Record retention:** keep leave records for the legally required period.
- Anything contested or ambiguous → HR/Legal before acting.

### 7. During absence

Keep the absence controlled without intruding on protected time.

- **Track status:** record actual start, and any extension request against policy limits.
- **Minimal contact:** respect that protected/personal leave is not working time; don't route work to someone on leave.
- **Handle changes:** early return, extension, or conversion to another leave type re-enters the workflow at the affected step (re-check policy and pay).

### 8. Return-to-work

Make the return deliberate, not an unannounced reappearance.

- **Confirm the return date** ahead of time; flag no-shows or silent extensions for follow-up.
- **Reinstatement:** restore the same or an equivalent role where protected leave requires it.
- **Fitness-for-duty / clearance** where the leave type and law require it (medical, extended absence) — *verify against local law.*
- **Re-onboarding:** for long absences, brief the returner on changes, restore access/tooling, and consider a phased/graduated return.
- **Update records:** close the leave, reconcile actual dates against approved, and settle final balances.

### 9. Tracking and reporting

Keep balances trustworthy and surface patterns.

- **Balance accuracy:** every leave posts to the record; balances and accruals reconcile.
- **Absence patterns:** monitor for trends (e.g., recurring Monday sick days) — a pattern is a signal, route it to [[employee-engagement]] or HR, don't act punitively from the tracker.
- **Liability & planning:** report accrued-leave liability and upcoming leave for workforce planning.
- **Audit trail:** the leave record is timestamped and complete for every request — approved *and* denied.

## Red Flags / Common Mistakes

| Red flag | Why it's a problem | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Request sits unacknowledged | Disengagement and HR complaints; the "silent no" | Acknowledge on intake with a decision date |
| Approving before classifying the leave type | Wrong policy, wrong pay, missed statutory notice | Classify type first; everything else follows |
| Manager denies a protected leave for coverage | Illegal interference with a statutory right | Policy owns entitlement; manager owns only feasibility |
| Protected leave not recognized as protected | Missed notices, retaliation exposure, penalties | Treat as protected until confirmed otherwise |
| Decision made on manager judgment, not policy | Inconsistent, discriminatory, indefensible | Decide strictly against written policy; cite the basis |
| No coverage plan for an approved absence | The absence becomes a team crisis | Assign coverage and hand off before the leave starts |
| Work routed to someone on protected leave | Violates the leave; erodes trust | Respect leave as non-working time; delegate elsewhere |
| Over-collecting medical/personal documentation | Privacy/compliance violation in itself | Collect only what law/policy permits |
| Accrual/pay treatment guessed, not set by type | Mis-payment and balance errors | Derive pay and accrual from the classified type |
| Return-to-work with no confirmed date or clearance | No-shows, unsafe returns, lost reinstatement rights | Confirm date, reinstate to equivalent role, clear if required |

## Exit criteria

- [ ] Request captured with employee, dates, notice given, and acknowledged on intake with a decision date.
- [ ] Leave classified into a policy category (PTO, sick, parental, unpaid, statutory/protected), not just the employee's label.
- [ ] Eligibility and balance/entitlement checked; decision (approve/deny/partial) made against written policy with the basis recorded.
- [ ] Manager approval obtained and a coverage plan is in place; approval confirmed to the employee in writing with dates, type, pay, and return date.
- [ ] Pay treatment, accrual impact, statutory pay, and benefits continuity set correctly and sent to payroll before cutoff.
- [ ] Statutory designation/notices issued within the required window; documentation collected within legal limits; records retained per law *(verify against local law)*.
- [ ] Return-to-work confirmed — date set, reinstatement to same/equivalent role, any required clearance completed, leave closed and dates reconciled.
- [ ] Leave record complete and timestamped (approved and denied requests); balances reconciled; any absence pattern routed to the right owner, not punished from the tracker.
