# Hiring

## Overview

Full-cycle hiring from an approved need to a signed offer and a clean handoff to onboarding. The core principle: **a structured, documented process makes better hires** — SHRM finds structured hiring makes good hires roughly twice as likely and cuts time-to-hire by ~35% versus informal approaches. Every stage produces an artifact and a go/no-go decision so nothing advances on gut feel alone.

Benchmark reality (SHRM 2025): a non-executive hire averages ~45 days and ~$5,475; ~40% of senior roles take 90+ days. Plan timelines against seniority, not hope.

## When to use

- A hiring manager has an approved headcount/budget need and wants to open a role.
- An active pipeline needs a defined screening → interview → offer flow.
- You are extending, negotiating, or closing an offer and need the sequence.
- You are running a background/reference check and closing out unselected candidates.

## When NOT to use

- The role is not yet approved or budgeted → resolve the business case and budget first; do not open a req against unfunded headcount.
- The candidate has accepted and you are setting them up → use [[employee-onboarding]].
- You are managing an existing employee's growth, reviews, or exit → use [[employee-engagement]].
- Pure contractor/vendor procurement with no employment relationship.

## Workflow

1. **Requisition & approval.** Draft the requisition: title, department, hiring reason (backfill vs. growth), level, budgeted salary range, must-have vs. nice-to-have skills, and reporting line. Route through the approval chain (hiring manager → finance/HR → exec if required). **Do not source until the req is approved in writing.**
2. **Intake & scorecard.** Hold a kickoff with the hiring manager. Define the success profile: 4–6 evaluation competencies, target outcomes for the first 6–12 months, and a shared scorecard. Agree the interview panel and each interviewer's focus area up front to avoid overlap.
3. **Job description & sourcing.** Write an inclusive, outcome-focused JD from the scorecard. Publish to the agreed channels (job boards, referrals, agencies, direct sourcing). Track source-of-hire so you can invest where quality candidates actually come from.
4. **Screening pipeline.** Apply a consistent screen: resume/application review against must-haves, then a structured recruiter phone screen (compensation expectations, work authorization, motivation, availability). Move only qualified candidates forward; disposition the rest promptly with a reason code.
5. **Interview planning & execution.** Run structured interviews — the same core questions and scorecard for every candidate at a given stage. Brief interviewers, assign competencies, and schedule efficiently to protect the candidate experience. Collect written scorecards **before** any debrief so opinions aren't anchored.
6. **Debrief & selection.** Hold a panel debrief; each interviewer submits their scorecard and a hire/no-hire recommendation independently first. Reach a documented decision against the success profile — not "culture fit" vibes. Identify the finalist and a backup.
7. **Offer management.** Confirm the approved level and comp band, then extend a verbal offer, followed by a written offer letter. Negotiate within the approved band; escalate exceptions for approval before promising anything. Set a decision deadline and stay in contact through it.
8. **Background & references.** After offer acceptance (and only with candidate consent), run the agreed background check and reference checks per role and per local law. Treat adverse findings through a consistent, documented adjudication step — never an ad-hoc reject.
9. **Close-out & handoff.** Once cleared, confirm the start date and trigger [[employee-onboarding]] with the offer details, level, manager, and start date. Disposition every remaining candidate (notify, thank, keep-warm for silver-medalists), close the requisition, and record final metrics (time-to-fill, source-of-hire, cost).

## Red Flags / Common Mistakes

| Red flag | Why it's a problem | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing before the req is approved | Wasted work, comp promises you can't keep, legal exposure | Approve req + budget in writing first |
| Unstructured, interviewer-improvised questions | Bias, inconsistent signal, non-comparable candidates | Structured questions + shared scorecard per stage |
| Debrief before scorecards are submitted | Loudest/most senior voice anchors the group | Independent written scorecards first, then debrief |
| "Culture fit" as the deciding reason | Proxy for bias; not job-related | Decide against the success profile and competencies |
| Offer extended above band "to close" | Pay equity and budget breakage | Negotiate within band; escalate exceptions first |
| Background check run without consent / inconsistently | Legal violation (e.g., FCRA-style rules), discrimination risk | Consent + consistent, documented adjudication |
| Rejected candidates left in limbo | Damages employer brand, loses silver-medalists | Disposition promptly with reason codes |
| Requisition left open after fill | Skews metrics, keeps candidates applying | Close the req and record final metrics |

## Exit criteria

- [ ] Requisition approved in writing (title, level, budgeted range, reason) before sourcing began.
- [ ] Success profile / scorecard defined and used by every interviewer.
- [ ] Every advanced candidate has a completed structured screen and interview scorecards on file.
- [ ] Selection decision documented against the success profile, with a named finalist and backup.
- [ ] Written offer accepted within the approved comp band (exceptions approved on record).
- [ ] Background and reference checks completed with consent and consistent adjudication.
- [ ] Start date confirmed and [[employee-onboarding]] triggered with offer + manager + start date.
- [ ] All non-selected candidates dispositioned; requisition closed; metrics recorded (time-to-fill, source-of-hire, cost).
