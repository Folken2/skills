# Employee Onboarding

## Overview

Turn an accepted offer into a productive, retained employee. The core principle: **structured onboarding is a developmental arc, not a checklist of first-day paperwork.** Gallup finds employees with exceptional onboarding are ~2.6x more likely to be very satisfied at work, and SHRM ties strong onboarding to large retention gains. The manager — not HR — owns ramp; HR owns structure, compliance, and access.

An unprepared day 1 is the most common and most preventable onboarding failure. Front-load the work.

## When to use

- A candidate has accepted and you need to provision and prepare before their start date.
- You are running day 1 / first week / first month for a new hire.
- You are setting or reviewing 30-60-90 day milestones.
- You are provisioning, changing, or planning the eventual removal of a new hire's access.

## When NOT to use

- The role isn't filled yet (still sourcing/interviewing/offer) → use [[hiring]].
- The hire is past ramp and you're running ongoing 1:1s, reviews, or growth plans → use [[employee-engagement]].
- A departure is actually happening now (final day, full access revocation, exit interview) → run the exit path in [[employee-engagement]]; this skill only *prepares* the access lifecycle.

## Workflow

### Pre-start (1–2 weeks before day 1)
1. **Paperwork & compliance.** Collect signed offer, employment eligibility/tax forms, and policy acknowledgements before day 1 so the first day isn't consumed by admin. Enroll in payroll and benefits.
2. **Equipment.** Order and image hardware; ship to remote hires with buffer time. Confirm it arrives before the start date.
3. **Accounts & access (provision).** Create identity, email, and role-based system access on the **principle of least privilege**. Record every system granted — this list is the source of truth for the eventual offboarding revocation. Stage accounts to activate on day 1, not before.
4. **Workspace & welcome.** Prepare desk/seat or remote workspace; send a warm welcome email with day-1 logistics (start time, location/link, dress, first-day agenda, who to ask for). Brief the team that a new person is joining.

### Day 1 (keep it to essentials)
5. **Orientation.** Cover the roadmap: mission, org, policies, tools, and where to find help. Confirm login and equipment work. Resist front-loading detailed training — spread it across week 1 and month 1.
6. **Introductions & manager 1:1.** Manager holds a day-1 1:1 to set tone, share the 30-60-90 expectations in writing, and make key introductions. Confirm the new hire knows where documentation and help live.

### First week
7. **Buddy assignment.** Assign an onboarding buddy (a peer, not the manager) for informal questions and social integration.
8. **Training & shadowing.** Schedule role training and shadowing of teammates. Focus the week on *context* — how the org works, who does what, how work flows — over output.

### 30-60-90 day milestones
9. **30 days — learn.** Manager check-in: how they feel, what's confusing, whether tools/access are complete, early feedback, and agreed 60-day expectations. Fix any access or tooling gaps now.
10. **60 days — contribute.** New hire is delivering with less supervision; review progress against expectations, adjust, and set 90-day goals.
11. **90 days — own.** Full-ownership review: are success-profile expectations met? Confirm the role is on track and fold the employee into the ongoing lifecycle via [[employee-engagement]].

### Offboarding prep (access lifecycle)
12. **Close the loop.** Because you recorded every system at step 3, offboarding readiness is a maintained artifact: keep the access list current as access changes so that, at departure, revocation is a complete, auditable checklist rather than a scramble. Never let access outlive the need.

## Red Flags / Common Mistakes

| Red flag | Why it's a problem | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment/accounts not ready on day 1 | Signals disorganization; kills early momentum | Provision 1–2 weeks ahead; verify before start |
| Day 1 crammed with detailed training | Overwhelms; low retention | Day 1 = essentials; spread training over weeks 1–4 |
| HR treated as ramp owner | Manager disengagement, poor role clarity | Manager owns ramp; HR owns structure/compliance |
| 30-60-90 expectations only verbal | Ambiguity, no measurable progress | Put milestones in writing on day 1 |
| Over-provisioned "just in case" access | Security and audit risk | Least privilege; grant only what the role needs |
| No record of granted access | Offboarding misses systems; orphaned accounts | Maintain a per-hire access inventory from day 1 |
| No buddy / no check-ins | Isolation, early attrition | Assign a buddy; run 30/60/90 check-ins |

## Exit criteria

- [ ] All pre-start paperwork, payroll, and benefits enrollment completed before day 1.
- [ ] Equipment delivered and verified working; workspace ready; welcome email sent.
- [ ] Accounts/access provisioned on least privilege, with a recorded per-hire access inventory.
- [ ] Day-1 orientation delivered; manager 1:1 held; 30-60-90 expectations given in writing.
- [ ] Onboarding buddy assigned; first-week training/shadowing scheduled.
- [ ] 30-, 60-, and 90-day check-ins held and documented; access/tooling gaps resolved.
- [ ] Access inventory maintained and current so offboarding revocation is a complete checklist.
- [ ] Employee transitioned into the ongoing lifecycle via [[employee-engagement]] at day 90.
